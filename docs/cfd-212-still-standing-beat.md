**Does a forgone option weigh anything once you have to live beside it?**

| --- | --- | --- |

banking was the branch that cost an extra navigation. *"There is no edit that would
make a branch more available than already-on-screen, named, and priced."* Legibility is
not the constraint. **Living past the choice is.**

## THE STOP — RULED, David, 2026-09-04

> *"Take the second staked run as the stop, and don't try to fix the 36%."*

**And the reason is a distinction this beat had collapsed.** David:

> *"The exhaustion path isn't the branch ending the sitting. Pressing on and losing
> doesn't end anything; running out of affordable sends does, two decisions later,
> after the player has lived past the fork with the forgone road standing. That's the
> property the card exists to test, and it holds. The board ends because you spent
> everything, which is a different thing from the board ending because you chose."*

**The draft counted the exhaustion path as a cost of the recommendation — 36% of
sittings on which "a branch does end the sitting after all."** That was wrong, and it
is corrected rather than deleted so the next reader can see which reading was
discarded. Losing the press-on ends nothing. The player keeps playing, with the
ending forgone and visible, and the sitting closes later because the wallet is empty.
**Ending by spending everything is not the fork ending the sitting.** The one new
system survives the exhaustion path intact.

**What David did push on is the FRAMING, and it is now a Kill line rather than a
polish note:**

> *"'Nothing lit and the sitting still running' is a dead screen even when it's
> diegetically correct, and it's the outcome you're most likely to hit at 36%. The
> stop firing on exhaustion means the player sees an ending; it doesn't mean they see
> why. Worth checking the exhaustion stop reads as a consequence rather than as the
> board giving up, since a player who can't tell those apart will report the second."*

**So the exhaustion ending must say what spent the wallet.** A player who cannot tell
"you ran out" from "the board stopped working" will report the second, and that report
would be about the ending rather than about the forgone road — which is the one thing
this card cannot afford to have its sitting spent on. The words go in
`notice().writing`/`.blocked`, because those are the only two fields the page renders.

### Why this was an open call, and what §7.2 required of it

§7.2 (`docs/mechanisms-recommitted.md:578`) requires that the stop exist and
be honest, and that the quick path be real. **C13's stop *was* the fork.** Remove that
and the stop has no home. There is no version of the one new system that leaves the
question open, so the answer is part of the same system rather than a second one.

David's ruling 1 of 2026-09-03 anticipates this and settles the category in advance:

> "The one new system has to be that branches don't end the sitting, and nothing else.
> That's a big enough change on its own, and it's the first board in this lineage where
> the run continues past a decision, which means the corridor has to hold across a
> state it has never been in. Worth expecting the walk to need work, and worth not
> calling that a second system when it does."

**Relocating the stop is the walk needing work.** It is the same system's other half:
the branches stop ending the sitting, so something else has to. Counting it as a second
system would mean the one new system cannot be built at all.

### The recommendation: the stop moves to the SECOND STAKED short run

**The rule, stated once, and every table in this document is written to it:**

> **The larder covers a STAKED short run, and it covers exactly one per sitting. The
> second one closes the sitting.**

- The opening send is staked (`stakeOf()` is 2, `sim.js:104-106`). It comes home short —
  **staked short run 1.** The larder covers it, the board arms, **the fork opens** and
  the larder latch is set.
- The fork resolves — bank, press-and-pay, or press-and-lose — and **play continues in
  every case.** The arm clears on all three so that play can continue; the latch is
  what stops the fork re-opening.
- **A later STAKED send comes home short — staked short run 2 — and the sitting ends.**

**The press-on is not a staked run and cannot spend the larder, and that is measured,
not asserted.** `commitPress` writes `provisions: 0, toll: 0` into the run object
(`sim.js:410-411`), and `commitHome`'s loss line is
`s.marksLost += run.provisions + run.toll` (`:439`). Driven at HEAD: after an opening
short send followed by a lost press-on, `marksLost` is **2**, not 4. **Nothing was put
up, so there is nothing for the larder to cover.**

**This is the rule an earlier cut of this beat stated two incompatible ways** — as
*"exactly one short run"* in the recommendation and as *press-on-short continues* in the
Ends table. Under the first reading a lost press-on stops the sitting, which is C13 with
a longer sentence and removes the one new system from the branch that needs it most.
**That reading is refused by name.** The rule above is the only one, it is stated in the
mechanism rather than only in a table, and the Ends table below agrees with it.

**Four reasons, and the first is the strongest.**

1. **The fiction is already written, on the parent, on the tile, and the stake is what
   carries it.** `PRESS_COST` at `sim.js:86` is *"The larder covered it once. It will
   not cover it twice."* The larder is what covers a shortfall on something you put up.
   You put up 2 on the opening send and the larder covered it; you put up nothing on the
   press-on, so it is not the larder's to cover, and the next thing you put up is the
   thing it refuses. **The sentence stays literally true, and it becomes the board's
   rule rather than one branch's threat.** *(What changes is what it threatens — see
   ARGUE 1.)*
2. **It adds no verb.** No new can-do, no new tile, no new place, no new number. The
   corridor's count is untouched.
3. **It guarantees a stretch of play in which the forgone branch is visible.** That
   stretch is the entire instrument. A stop that could fire immediately after the fork
   would reproduce C13 with extra steps.
4. **The pacing is the same on all three branches**, precisely because the press-on is
   unstaked. A banker, a press-winner and a press-loser all need a second staked short
   run. There is no branch that is quietly longer than the others, and no budget
   asymmetry for a player to feel and be unable to name.

**What it costs, stated rather than discovered.**

- **The player no longer taps the ending**, and §7.2 says *"the stop is what makes
  length opt-in."* Under this proposal the length is a draw, not a choice. Derived from
  the shipped chance (exactly `0.64`, so a short run is exactly `0.36` — measured off
  `notice("mosswake").chance` at HEAD): the number of staked sends to the second staked
  short run is negative-binomial with r=2, so the **mean is 5.56**, and the tail is real
  — **40.9%** of sittings run past five staked sends, **7.6%** past ten, **1.2%** past
  fifteen. At four taps a run on the shipped walk — post Mosswake, SEND, post the
  consist, Home she comes (`index.html:458-462`) — the median sitting is a couple of
  dozen taps and the long tail is a chore. **This is the honest cost and it is the
  reason the call is David's.**
- **The fork becomes once per sitting, by construction.** On the parent it can re-open
  after a paid press-on — CFD-210 measured it and filed it as an unregistered rider
  (`docs/cfd-210-beat.md:102`, table at `:111-115`), driven here and confirmed: after a
  paid press-on and one more short run, `liveCanDos()` is `Collect.` plus
  `ROLL HER OUT. 64.` again. Under this proposal the second staked short run is the stop
  instead. **Those are the same event and cannot both happen**, so the parent's re-fork
  is *replaced*, not inherited.
- **The latch has to gate `canCollect()`, not only `canPress()`.** `canCollect()`
  (`sim.js:138-144`) tests only `stopped` / `armed` / `collected` / `consistAt`, so a
  player who pressed on and lost, and never banked, would see `Collect.` light again at
  any later arm. That is the fork opening twice and it is a Kill line. **Gating the
  parent's `canCollect` is a deliberate overturn of a passed system — see the *On this
  sitting* table.**

### The alternatives, costed honestly

**Alternative 1 — a stop verb that is always available.** The player ends when they
choose, so length is opt-in by construction and no state can strand them.

**Refused, and the refusal is measurable.** A stop that is *always* available is a
second live can-do in **every** post-fork state. `/dawnspur-halt/` pre-walk offered two
and failed — *"Two opening can-dos; he stopped"* (`KILLS.md:42`) — and every board that
has passed since offers one, two only at the fork. A permanent two is a bigger
structural change than the one this card is for.

**Alternative 2 — exhaustion: no affordable send and nothing to collect.** No verb, no
counter, and the world simply runs out.

**Measured, it is not a stop.** A staked send is worth 0.64 x 14 minus the stake of 2 =
**+6.96 marks in expectation**, so a playing wallet drifts up, not down. Once any run has
paid, the opening float of 3 has become 15, and going broke needs **seven consecutive
short sends** — probability **0.078%**. A condition that fires at eight parts in ten
thousand is not an ending. **But it is reachable in exactly one place, and this card puts
a player there** — the next section, and it is a separate call from where the stop lives.

**Alternative 3 — arming-and-triggering: the second staked short run ARMS the ending,
and one can-do closes it.** *This is the shape canon itself names, and an earlier cut of
this beat did not consider it.* §7.2's companion, `docs/mechanisms-recommitted.md:595-599`:
*"Arming-and-triggering ... topping the terrace arms the ending, the qualifying cargo
triggers it — is the shape that gives a builder their payoff without ending the sitting
under a player who came for the run."*

**It is NOT alternative 1, and the difference is measurable.** The verb exists **only in
the armed state**, and `canSend()` returns false while armed (`sim.js:125`) — which is
exactly why the parent's own arm shows two can-dos rather than three. So an armed ending
with one can-do leaves the corridor at **one**, everywhere, permanently. Driven at HEAD,
the parent's arm lights exactly `Collect.` plus `ROLL HER OUT. 64.`; alternative 3 is
that state with the press branch latched off.

**What it costs, and it is not nothing:**

- **It is a new can-do face and a new verb**, which the recommendation's reason 2 does
  not spend. It is the closest thing in this document to a second system, and David's
  ruling 1 is the thing it has to be argued against.
- **It needs a tile.** At the armed-ending state Mosswake is free only under the branch
  rewrite above, and the consist is free. Both are available; neither is obviously
  right, and picking one is a design call this beat does not make.
- **It does not remove the floor.** The dead screen below is still reachable, because a
  player stuck at 1 mark can never reach a second staked short run to arm anything. See
  the next section.

**§7.2 is TRADED, not satisfied, and that is the ruling David is being asked for.**
§7.2 has two testable clauses (`:591-599`): *a quick path must exist and be honest*, and
*the stop is what makes length opt-in*. The recommendation keeps the first — banking is
the quick path, honest, `Collect.` the instant the board arms — and **voids the second**:
under it, nothing the player taps ends the sitting. Alternative 3 keeps both. **So
ruling for the recommendation is an amendment to §7.2, not a cost absorbed under it, and
it should be recorded as one.**

### The state the corridor has never been in, and it is a dead screen

**Measured at HEAD.** The opening float is 3 and `stakeOf()` is 2, so the first staked
send leaves **1 mark**. Driven with forced dice: a short first run arms the board at
**1 mark**, `canSend()` false, `canPress()` true, `canCollect()` true — the fork is what
unsticks the player, which is the asymmetry David asked to be checked before he signed
CFD-210.

Now take the press-on and lose it. On the parent the sitting ends there. **Under this
card it does not**, and the player is left at 1 mark with the ending forgone. Probed on
the shipped board:

```
createBoard({marks: 0}).liveCanDos()  ->  []      canSend false
createBoard({marks: 1}).liveCanDos()  ->  []      canSend false
createBoard({marks: 2}).liveCanDos()  ->  [mosswake: "SEND. 64."]
```

**Nothing is lit. The sitting is running and there is nothing in it** — and it is
permanent, because no staked send can ever be afforded, so the second-staked-short-run
stop can never fire either. Worse, Mosswake's fallthrough notice in that state
(`sim.js:265`) reads *"The consist is not home."* while the consist **is** home; that
sentence is unreachable on the parent and becomes reachable here.

**The frequency, conditioned correctly.** The dead screen is reached when the opening
send comes home short and the player then presses on and loses. Of sittings that reach
the opening arm and press, **36%** land on it — the first short run has already happened,
so only the press-on's own draw is left. Across all sittings played by a player who
presses at the opening arm, it is **12.96%**. *(An earlier cut printed the 12.96%
against the conditional denominator. Both figures are right; the pairing was not, and
36% is the one that argues for the clause.)*

**The floor clause is RULED IN — it is what closes the dead screen — and the two
alternatives below are refused.** What is NOT ruled in is the draft's framing of the
floor as a cost: see the ruling at the head of this section. The floor closes a state
in which nothing is lit; it does not make the fork end the sitting.

1. **The stop covers it, evaluated only when nothing is in the air.** State the floor as:
   the sitting ends when **the fork is spent, the consist is HOME, no run is out, and no
   affordable send exists.** Both clauses are the same sentence in the fiction — the
   larder is empty and nothing more is coming home.

   **The timing is load-bearing and an earlier cut left it out.** Evaluated eagerly —
   "whenever `canSend()` is false" — the floor fires **mid-flight on the most common path
   through the bank branch.** Driven: banking leaves marks 2, `stakeOf()` is 2, so the
   very next SEND leaves **0** with the consist away. Probed in that state, `canSend()`
   is false and the only live can-do is `Home she comes. 64.` — so an eager floor stops
   the sitting with a run still out and that can-do never tappable. **Every bank-branch
   sitting passes through that frame.**

   **Cost, and it is a real one: on the press-lose-at-the-opening-arm path a branch does
   end the sitting after all** — the thing the new system exists to remove — for **36%**
   of sittings that press at that arm. It ends by exhaustion rather than by the choice,
   and the board can say so in words, but that player gets C13's experience with a longer
   sentence. **This is the honest recommendation, not a clean one, and David should see
   it as such.**
2. **Raise the opening float.** A float of 4 leaves 2 after the first send, so a lost
   press-on leaves a player who can still send. It is the cleanest fix and it is
   **refused here**: 4 is not in the parent's set of literals, and the float of 3 is
   load-bearing in the parent's own signature argument — it is what makes the stake
   asymmetry bite exactly when it matters. Named rather than omitted, because refusing
   it silently is how this project loses decisions. **David can overrule this; the
   orchestrator recommends against.**
3. **Gate the press-on on affordability** — only offer it when losing it would leave the
   player able to play. **Refused, measured:** at the arm reached from the opening send
   the player has 1 mark, so the branch would be dark on the most common path to the
   fork, and the fork would stop existing where it matters most.

**Say what would make this stop.** If, in argument or in build, moving the stop turns
out to require a second new system — a new verb, a new tile, a new number, a second
counter the player has to read — then the one new system cannot be built on this parent
and the card should say so and stop, rather than ship a board with two new things on it.
**A rendered refusal is an acceptable outcome here.** Note that alternative 3 is exactly
that boundary: it is a new verb, and ruling for it is ruling that the new verb is worth
the §7.2 clause it buys back.
